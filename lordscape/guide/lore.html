Lore

The story of the world: the Maker and His Words, the one who fell, the Church that keeps the faith, and the sea, stone and sky that still remember how the world was spoken.

The Maker

Before the world there was only the Hush, a silence with nothing in it. Then Ordan the Maker spoke, and what He spoke was. His name means the beginning, the point a thing starts from.

The Eight Words

Ordan made the world with eight great Words:

Word What it said
Will that anything should be
Gale, Tide, Stone, Ember the four the world is built of
Surge that it should move
Dread that it should end
Wrath never spoken aloud

The marks carved on old altars, and the sigils a mage carries, are these Words written down. Every altar stands where one of them first rang out (see Inscription and Magic).

The Andswaru

Before the world, Ordan spoke the Andswaru (one of them is an Andswar): great spirits who answer His Words, taking each one up and carrying it on, the way a note is sung back in a round. The world's shaping was their answering. The Andswar of Stone raised the mountains; the Andswar of Tide drew the sea. They still answer, faintly. Brother Oswin at Orison Tarn has spent twenty years listening to them.

Why He is quiet

Ordan has finished speaking. The world is His last sentence, still ringing. He answers prayer, but in the world's own voice (a warmth, a steadied hand, a wound closing), never in words.

The Fallen

Unwyrd, the Unsayer, was the first and greatest of the Andswaru, the one who answered Will. He grew proud: he wanted to speak a Word of his own, not only answer one. He reached for the Word Ordan had kept back, Wrath, the Word that ends things, meaning to speak it himself.

No voice but the Maker's could speak it, and it broke Unwyrd's voice as he said it. Now he can't say anything; he can only unsay. Where he passes, Words come undone: grass unsays itself to ash, a creature's gentleness unsays itself to rage, a name is forgotten.

  • The Blight in the east is the scar of his fall: land he unsaid.
  • The Hollow are his church. They call the silence holy and work to bring back the Hush: defacing Ordan's marks, stealing His Words, cutting the tongues out of bells.
  • Orcs, goblins and trolls were ordinary hill folk once, until Unwyrd unsaid their kindness. The Church still argues over whether they can be saved.
  • His champions carry tainted artifacts, holy things he has unsaid. Those artifacts can be reborn (see Rebirth below).
  • He is not dead, and he is not in the world: he lies beneath it. The deep places are quieter than they should be.

The Unspoken Word

Senna's grandmother told of a Word no one may speak, and her tale is true. The tablet from the Will altar and the firecallers' shard don't hold the Word itself, only its shape: enough to call on Wrath without speaking it, in Ordan's own voice rather than your own. Loremaster Ithiel teaches you to pray it rather than say it. That is why only the faithful may cast the Wrath spells. (The quest: The Unspoken Word.)

The Church of the Answer

The Church teaches that every life is meant to answer Ordan's Word, as the Andswaru do: to take up what He spoke and carry it on. Prayer is answering aloud.

  • The Cathedral of the First Answer stands on its spur above Athelbury, where the Church says the first Andswar answered. It holds the high altar, where the rites of rebirth are done, along with a font, a cloister, an almonry for the poor and a pilgrims' hostel. The Archprelate keeps it.
  • The Conclave of the Word, in the city below, is the Church's college, where the Words are studied and magic is taught. High Magister Ysolde keeps it.
  • The orders are the Church's monastic orders, each keeping one Word: the Choir of the Air (Gale), the Keepers of the Mere (Tide), the Hewn (Stone), the Storm-Wardens (Surge) and the Firekeepers (Ember).
  • The Seminary, now a ruin on the Will altar's hill, was the Church's first school. The Hollow burned it a century ago.
  • The Undercroft, the catacombs under Athelbury's old town, is where the Hollow hide what they steal from the Church. Its keeper, the Reliquarian, has sewn her own mouth shut.
  • Ranks. The Archprelate heads the Church; prelates oversee regions, vicars serve the villages, and brothers and sisters keep the monasteries. The Vigil is the Church's armed guard (one of them is a vigilant), and the pilgrims and novices walk its roads.
  • The Round is the Church's sign: a circle broken at the top, where the Word comes in.

The saints

Saints are those whose lives answered perfectly. Their relics still carry an echo of the Word, which is why relics can bless.

  • Saint Aldis the Lamplighter kept the Ashgate waystation's lamp burning through the first winter of the Blight.
  • Saint Ormund of the Anvil was a smith who re-forged a Hollow blade into a ploughshare and was martyred for it. He is the patron of smiths and of rebirth.
  • Saint Veyla of the Mere drowned rather than let the Hollow take the Drowned Chapel's bell. Her deacon held the door while she drowned. The Hollow taught him to forget why, and he holds it still, under the water.
  • Saint Corrin the Watchful was the vigilant who held the Stormcrown's pass alone.
  • Saint Brannagh was a hedge-healer of the old forest, patron of herbalists and the sick. Her shrine stands in the Heartwood, where Will was first answered (by Unwyrd, before he fell).

Prayer

Anyone may pray. Devotions are prayers you hold, and they drain your prayer points while they last; invocations are short, strong prayers, spoken once. Every prayer is said aloud, and its light shows as you say it. Altars and shrines restore your prayer points, and the cathedral's high altar fills you past your usual maximum. You train Prayer by offering bones, scattering the Fallen's cinders, going on pilgrimage to the seven shrines and doing the almonry's work. See Prayer for every devotion and invocation.

Rebirth

Holy artifacts can be reborn. First the artifact is unmade and re-forged at an anvil (Smithing), then consecrated at the high altar with sigils, holy water and a saint's relic (Prayer and Inscription). A reborn artifact carries one blessing at a time, and rebirth can change it. Tainted artifacts, the Fallen's champions' drops, can't be worn at all until they have been reborn once.

The first was Ormund's Hammer: his hammer was lost when the Hollow sacked the Seminary, and a tainted blade in the Hushed Prior's crypt was forged from it. Recover it, re-forge it, bless it, and the Archprelate names you to the rite (Born Again).

The Hearthgate

The stone arch outside Ashwick's south gate is older than the town, which grew up beside it. When the walls went up, nobody would wall it in. The Church has never quite decided what it is, and the builders don't much care. Through it, each traveller finds a plot of their own, and its light is always hearth-light, as if something on the far side were keeping a fire in for you. Aldous Plumb, the master builder beside it, knows it has a name, and that a mage who speaks it can call the gate open from anywhere (Hearth and Home).

The sea and the Andswar of Tide

  • The Grey Reach is what the Andswar of Tide drew when Ordan spoke the sea: the sea is still answering. The Tide order at Tideholm listens to it and rings its bell at every turn of the tide.
  • The Heart of Tide, on Tide's isle at the edge of the charts, is where the Andswar of Tide first answered, and still answers loudest: a pool that turns by itself, kept by the Warden of the Heart. The Leviathan coils round the isle. It isn't Fallen, only old, and it is set against anything that would disturb the Heart. Whoever comes that far and kneels hears the sea answer them, and nobody can ever say what it said (The Tide's Answer).
  • Saltgrave, the drowned kingdom: its last king swore an oath to hold the land against the sea, and the sea came in the night he swore it. The Tidebound King still keeps that oath in the Drowned Deep below his palace, and his flagship, the Drowned Galleon, still sails with her dead crew at the guns. Holding back an answer is the one thing the sea won't allow.
  • The Vine Temple on Greenshroud was the old kingdom's temple to Tide's answer. The jungle took it, and the Vine Matriarch grew in its heart.

Words spoken together

  • Bound sigils. Ordan didn't speak His Words one at a time, and where two met in the making the world shows it. Wind over water is Mist, water in stone the Mire of the low country, wind with fire in it Smoke, stone that remembers being fire Magma, and wind with the lightning in it Tempest. Dread and Wrath said together are the Dirge: the old kingdoms spoke it over their dead, and the Church still won't say it in a service. An inscriber binds two Words by speaking one at the other's altar over a stone that already carries the first.
  • Deep sigil stones. Sigil stone is the Hush's quiet, worn into rock by Ordan's voice. Deep under the Highfolds and on the Silent Amphitheatre's floor the voice went deeper, and the stone there is dark and glassy and takes a Word twice over.
  • Graven words. The old masons cut the Words into their stone with gravers of black bronze. Their last work was the Graven Colossus, a giant of dressed stone with all eight Words cut into it, set in the Masons' Vault under the Silent Amphitheatre. A graver prised out of it takes one Word into a piece of gear, and the Word answers only the hand that cut it. The Church calls it presumption; smiths and mages call it good work.
  • The Old Tongue. The old kingdoms never bound the Words into stones. They sang them two at once, the way a choir holds two notes. Their chorus is cut into the chorus stone on the Silent Amphitheatre's floor, its last verse broken off: the Buried King took it down into the Sunken Kingdom when the sand came (The Old Tongue).

The falling Words

  • Skystones. When Ordan spoke the world, not every Word struck the ground at once, and some are still falling. Every hour and a half or so, a shard of that first speaking comes down somewhere in the lands. Astronomer Sefa watches for them from the Stormcrown's storm tower. Miners chip skydust from them, and the first pick in a fresh one is said to be blessed.
  • The Fallen Words. The greater Words fall too, heavy with the metal they first named. Each comes down in the same place every time, where that metal's old seams were first struck: copper and tin together (as bronze was spoken) into the great crater that was Ashwick's quarry. You can see one coming, a light across the sky and the ground glowing where it will land. It lies ten minutes before it crumbles, and the next comes down in the same spot. The crater drinks the blow into the ground, and the Conclave keeps three channelers at every one. They turn that force along two veins through the earth: one to a forge on the crater's rim that never goes out (iron smelts true there), the other to a sigil vein that never runs dry. Those who work a fallen Word together find their picks ring truer, and those who stay to the end share its heart. (See Mining.)
  • Starstone. The rarest gem in the rock is a pale gold stone that holds a small, steady light. Miners say it turns up where a Word struck hardest.
  • The Lode. Under Cragmoot lie the first seams the Hewn ever worked, long since given up as worked out. But old seams remember being worked, and with a hopper, a water wheel and patience, the rubble still gives up ore.
